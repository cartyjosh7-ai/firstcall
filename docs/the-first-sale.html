01

Find someone to call

No lead scraper exists yet, so today the list is built by hand. Every source below is free and legal.

  1. Pick a trade and a city
    Start narrow — one trade, one city you know. First Call's live trades are HVAC, Roofing, Mold & Remediation, Cleaning & Janitorial, Auto & Wraps, Trucking & Equipment. A single-location, owner-led business is the best fit for Foundation — that's who the offer (and the pricing) is built for.
  2. Search Google Maps as a human, not a scraper
    Search "[trade] near [city]" and write down what you see — especially the businesses sitting below the map pack (top 3). They're losing the most calls to competitors and they're the easiest pitch, because the problem is visible to them the moment you say it out loud. This is manual research, not automated scraping — automating this against Google Maps breaks their terms of service and isn't something to build.
  3. Pull from directories
    Yellow Pages Canada, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, and each trade's own provincial association directory (e.g. a provincial HVAC or roofing contractor association) list name, phone, and website for free. Chamber of Commerce member directories work the same way, and skew toward already-established local operators.
  4. Run the real audit on each one before you call
    Use /audit on their actual website. It's a real scan — homepage, up to three interior pages, and robots.txt, scored across five categories — not a guess. Walking into a call with their real number already in hand is a stronger opener than any cold intro.
  5. Log it in the CRM the moment you know it exists
    Add it at /crm/leads/new — even before the first call. An added-by-hand lead starts warm; it flips to hot automatically the moment you log a contact against it, and stays that way until you mark it won or lost. This is what keeps a name from quietly falling through the cracks.

One legal note on phone calls

Canada's National Do Not Call List covers unsolicited calls to consumers — calling another business, about that business, is exempt. That's what a cold sales call to a trades company is. Still: the moment anyone asks not to be called again, stop and log it — good practice regardless of the exemption, and the contact log already gives you the place to record it.

02

The call

Confident and plain-spoken, allergic to hype — that's First Call's actual brand voice, not a script gimmick. Use it.

Opening — cold, by phone

You
"Hi, is this [owner name]? This is [you] with First Call — we help [trade] businesses in [city] show up first when someone searches for [trade] near me, on Google, on the map, and now in AI answers like ChatGPT. Got 60 seconds?"
If they say yes
"I actually ran a quick scan on your site before calling — you're at [score] out of 100 on visibility right now. Want me to walk you through what that means and where the gap is?"
Voicemail
"Hi [owner name], [you] with First Call. I ran a quick visibility check on [business] and found a few things costing you calls that are fixable in a day or two. Call me back at [number], or I'll try you again [day]."

Walking through the score

Use the score as a diagnostic, not a scare tactic — connect each gap to a real consequence, not a vague warning:

  • Maps — "This is the map with the top three results. Right now a competitor is sitting there instead of you for [search term] — that's a phone that rings for them, not you."
  • AI Overviews / AI Assistants — "When someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI answer who to call, it quotes whoever's content is structured so it can be quoted. Right now that's not you."
  • Voice — "More searches start as a spoken question every year. If your listing isn't complete and consistent everywhere, voice assistants skip you."
  • Website & Reviews — "This is your site's technical health and review signal — both feed directly into whether Google trusts you enough to rank you."

Never claim a specific rank, call volume, or revenue outcome — the audit measures signals, not guarantees, and the contract says so explicitly. That restraint is itself part of the pitch: every trades owner has already heard the guarantee pitch, and didn't believe it.

Presenting the offer

You
"Here's exactly what's fixable and how: Foundation is $2,799 to set up, then $139.99 a month, month-to-month — no contract lock-in. You get a senior strategist, not a junior account manager, and a plain-English report every month on calls, jobs, and visibility — not vanity metrics."

What's actually included: Business Profile + local optimization, a review acquisition system, core on-page work and LocalBusiness schema, foundational content, monthly reporting, and a named strategist who owns the account. Not included: paid ad spend, multi-location work, digital PR, or a rebuilt website from scratch.

Objections

Guarantee "Will I actually rank #1?"
"I won't promise a specific rank or call volume — nobody honest can, and our contract says so in writing. What I can tell you exactly is what we're fixing and why it moves the signals that decide who gets the call."
Scope "I actually need a new website."
"Foundation optimizes what you've already got — it doesn't include building a new site from scratch. If yours genuinely needs a rebuild, that's a separate conversation to scope — let's start with Foundation, and handle that piece on its own."
Price "I already have someone doing my Google stuff."
"Fair — can I ask what your Map Pack position is for your main service right now? [Listen.] That's exactly the kind of thing our monthly report tracks in plain numbers, so you can actually see whether what you're paying for is working."
Stall "Let me think about it."
Don't discount. If Foundation genuinely feels like too big a first step, offer the standalone Technical SEO Audit ($497 one-time) instead — a real, lower-commitment entry point, not a consolation prize.

03

Close it

Assume the sale, then make paying the easiest part of the call.

You
"Should I text you the payment link now, or would you rather I email it? Either way it's the same page — business name, website, and trade, then straight to checkout."
  1. Send the right package link
    /start defaults to Foundation. For another package, add ?package=growth, domination, or audit to the URL — same form, same flow, correct price on the other end.
  2. They pay on Stripe's hosted page
    First Call never touches a card number directly. The setup fee and the monthly retainer are billed together as one subscription — the setup fee charges once, the retainer continues monthly.
  3. Everything past this point is automatic
    Stripe confirms payment, the system runs the research and 90-day-plan agents in the background, saves the report, and mirrors the client into the CRM as won. Nothing for you to trigger manually — unless the report comes back flagged mock data (see the callout at the top of this page), in which case regenerate it before the client ever sees it.
  4. No working checkout yet? Take it manually
    Note the sale in the CRM as won yourself from /crm/leads/new, and arrange payment by invoice once Stripe is live. Don't let a real prospect sit on a broken checkout page wondering if it's you or them.

05

Deliver it

Foundation is senior-strategist-owned, not junior or outsourced. The day-by-day month-1 checklist already exists and is live — this is the plan behind it, plus what comes after.

Month 1 Days 1–30

The full day-by-day is already built and public at /playbook — kickoff and baselines, primary-category research, full GBP completeness, review acquisition live, citation audit, on-page and schema work, one real service+city page, tracking confirmed, and the month-1 report. Follow it in order; it's the actual execution checklist, not a summary of one.

  • Technical bar: Core Web Vitals — LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1, all three passing together.
  • Content bar (E-E-A-T): real photos, a named credentialed writer, accurate sourced facts — not generic template copy.
  • Don't skip: confirm robots.txt isn't blocking AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended) — a blocked crawler means invisible to AI answers, full stop.

Month 2 Days 31–60

  • Keep review velocity going — this compounds, it doesn't finish in month 1.
  • Close out any remaining citations from the Day 7–8 audit.
  • Build out service and location page architecture beyond the single page shipped in month 1.
  • Regular Google Business Profile posts and Q&A activity.
  • Iterate directly off what the month-1 report showed — don't run a generic month 2.

Month 3 Days 61–90

  • This is the typical window local ranking movement starts becoming visible — set that expectation with the client at kickoff, not as a surprise now.
  • Deepen content beyond the single foundational piece.
  • Confirm tracking and attribution are solid enough to trust the numbers in the report.
  • Have the honest conversation: stay on Foundation, or is this a genuine Growth fit now that there's a real baseline to scale from.

Every month, ongoing

The report is plain English: more visible? more calls? what's next? Calls, jobs, and visibility — never vanity metrics, never a fabricated case-study number. This is a stated commitment in Foundation's scope, not a nice-to-have — build the habit starting with client zero.
