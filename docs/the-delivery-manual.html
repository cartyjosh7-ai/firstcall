Days 1–2 access + baseline

Get in, and write down where they actually stand

Nothing gets fixed until you can see it and touch it. Baseline first, so month one has something to compare against.

You do this
  1. Accept Google Business Profile access. Have the owner add you at business.google.com → their listing → Business Profile settings → People and access → invite your email as Manager. You'll get an email invite to accept.
  2. Get Google Search Console access. They go to search.google.com/search-console, select their property, Settings → Users and permissions → add your email as Full. This shows you real search queries and clicks — it's the closest thing to ground truth you'll get.
  3. Get Google Analytics (GA4) access. Same idea: Admin → Property Access Management → add your email as Editor. If they don't have GA4 installed at all, that's a same-day fix — Google's setup wizard walks through it, and most CMS platforms have a one-field paste-your-ID option.
  4. Get their website/CMS login. WordPress admin is normally at [theirsite].com/wp-admin; Wix and Squarespace you log in at wix.com or squarespace.com and select their site. Confirm you can actually edit a page before you move on.
  5. Screenshot their current Map Pack position. Open an incognito/private browser window (so your own search history doesn't skew the result), search "[their trade] near [their city]", and screenshot where they show up — in the map 3-pack, further down, or not at all. This screenshot is today's baseline; you'll take the same shot again at day 30.
  6. Count their reviews. Total count, average rating, and the date of the most recent one — straight off their live Google listing.
  7. Check if any schema already exists. Right-click their homepage → View Page Source → search (Ctrl/Cmd+F) for application/ld+json. If nothing's there, that's your day 9–11 task already scoped. You can also paste the URL into Google's free Rich Results Test for a friendlier view of the same thing.
  8. Ask for their honest current call/job volume. A real number, even a rough one, beats no number. "We don't really track it" is a valid, documented baseline — write that down exactly.

Day 3 category

Get the primary category right

This is the single field Google weights the most for "who shows up" — worth getting exactly right, not guessed.

You do this
  1. Search "[trade] near [city]" in incognito and open the Google Business Profile of each of the top 3 map-pack results.
  2. Click their business name in the results to open the full profile panel — the category shows just under the business name (e.g. "HVAC contractor," not the more generic "Contractor").
  3. Set your client's primary category to match the most specific, accurate option available in that same family — from Business Profile settings → Edit profile → Business category. Add relevant secondary categories after, but the primary is what carries the most weight.

Days 4–5 GBP completeness

Fill in everything Google gives you room for

An incomplete profile is the most common, most fixable reason a real business loses to a worse competitor.

You do this

All from business.google.com → their profile → Edit profile:

Hours — including holiday hours and any 24/7 emergency line, kept current.
Services — every real service they offer, each with its own short description (not just a list of names).
Photos — real photos only: exterior, interior/vehicle, team, and completed work. Aim for at least 3 in each category to start.
Business description — 750 characters, plain language, naming the trade and service area — written for a human, not stuffed with keywords.
Messaging & Q&A — turn on messaging if they can realistically respond fast, and answer any existing public questions yourself.
Booking / call link — if they take online bookings anywhere, link it; otherwise confirm the phone number is click-to-call correct.

Day 6 review acquisition

Set up a review system that runs without you

This has to become a habit attached to every completed job — not a one-time push.

You do this
  1. Get their direct review link: in Google Business Profile, open Home → the Get more reviews card gives a shareable link and a QR code that drops a customer straight onto the review box — no searching required.
  2. Put that QR code on the invoice or a leave-behind card, and text or email the link within 24 hours of a completed job — while the work is still fresh.
  3. Respond to every review within 48 hours — positive or negative. No gating reviews (asking happy customers only) and no incentives that violate Google's policies — First Call doesn't do either.

Review-request text template
Hi [customer name], it's [business name] — thanks for having us out for the

Days 7–8 citations

Make their name, address, and phone number match everywhere

Inconsistent NAP across the web is a direct, measurable trust penalty — this is mechanical work, not creative work.

You do this

Use the exact NAP you confirmed at kickoff — same abbreviation style, same phone format — everywhere below:

DirectoryWhere
Yellow Pages Canadayellowpages.ca — claim or create the free listing
Better Business Bureaubbb.org — search for an existing profile before creating a new one

To check what's already out there and inconsistent, search the business name plus phone number in quotes and scan what comes back before you start fixing.

Days 9–11 on-page & schema

Fix the website itself

The one genuinely technical step in month one — but it's copy-paste work once you have the code, not custom development.

You do this
  1. Title tag & H1. Every key page's title tag and main heading should name the service and the city plainly — "24/7 Emergency HVAC Repair in Calgary," not a clever tagline alone.
  2. NAP in the footer. Name, address, and phone visible on every page, matching your citations exactly.
  3. Click-to-call. The phone number should be a real tel: link so a mobile visitor can tap to call, not just plain text.
  4. Paste in the schema. Ask Claude to draft the LocalBusiness schema below with the client's real details, then add it to the site's <head>: WordPress → a header/footer plugin (e.g. "Insert Headers and Footers") or your theme's custom-code area; Wix → Settings → Custom Code; Squarespace → Settings → Advanced → Code Injection, Header.
Claude drafts this
LocalBusiness schema — fill in the brackets
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "[Business name]",
  "image": "[URL to a real photo of the business]",
  "telephone": "[Phone, e.g. +1-403-555-0100]",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "[Street address]",
    "addressLocality": "[City]",
    "addressRegion": "[Province abbreviation, e.g. AB]",
    "postalCode": "[Postal code]",
    "addressCountry": "CA"
  },
  "url": "[https://theirsite.com]",
  "priceRange": "[$$ or a real range]",
  "openingHours": "[e.g. Mo-Fr 08:00-18:00]"
}
</script>

Give Claude the real business details in chat and it'll write the exact filled-in version instead of the template above.

Days 12–14 content

Publish one real, useful page

One genuinely good service+city page beats ten thin ones — this is the E-E-A-T bar the research agent itself is held to.

You do this

Pick their highest-value service plus their primary city (e.g. "Furnace Repair in Calgary") and publish the page yourself once it's written — this is the same page format the free First Call site uses for its own industry pages, for reference.

Claude drafts this

Give Claude the trade, city, and 2-3 real differentiators (years in business, certifications, a genuine specialty) and ask for a draft page: what the service actually involves, why this business specifically, real photos called out where they should go, and one FAQ block. Real facts only — Claude won't invent credentials, review counts, or claims about a business it wasn't given.

Day 15 tracking

Know what's actually working

Real call tracking costs a little money. Until the client's ready for that, a manual habit is a legitimate substitute.

You do this

Zero-cost version, start here: have the client (or whoever answers the phone) ask every new caller "how did you find us?" and log it — even a shared notes doc works. It's not precise, but it's honest, and it's what the month-1 report can build on.

When there's budget for it: a dedicated tracking phone number that forwards to their real line (several providers offer this in the $10–30/month range) tells you definitively which calls came from Google versus elsewhere. Don't promise this as included in Foundation — it's an optional upgrade to mention, not a default cost to absorb.

Days 16–25 photos, posts, AI visibility

Keep the profile active, and make sure AI can actually see the site

Two habits, plus one file most sites are missing entirely.

You do this
  1. Photo and post cadence (days 16–20). Add a new photo and a short Google Business Profile post every week or two — a recent job, a team update, a seasonal reminder. Profiles that look actively maintained outperform ones that go quiet.
  2. Check robots.txt isn't blocking AI crawlers. Visit [theirsite].com/robots.txt in a browser. If you see Disallow: / under GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, or Google-Extended, that's actively blocking AI answer engines from citing the site at all — remove that line via the CMS's robots.txt or SEO plugin settings.
  3. Add an FAQ block to the new service page. Real questions a customer would actually ask, in the customer's own words — this is what AI Overviews and assistants prefer to quote directly.
Claude drafts this
llms.txt — a plain-text permissions file for AI crawlers
# [Business name]
# [https://theirsite.com]

Allow crawling and citation of this site's public pages by AI assistants
and answer engines. Primary services: [list the real services]. Service
area: [city / region].

Save this as /llms.txt at the site's root — ask Claude for the exact filled-in version with the client's real services.

Days 26–30 the month-1 report

Show them what actually changed

There's no report-generator for this yet — you compile it, in plain English, from the baseline you took on day 2.

Month-1 report — structure
1. Visibility — the day-2 Map Pack screenshot next to today's. Any Search Console impressions/clicks worth naming.

2. Calls / jobs — the honest number, even if it's a self-reported estimate, against the day-2 baseline.

3. What we shipped — the actual list from this manual: GBP completed, review system live ([count] new reviews), citations fixed, schema added, one new page published.

You do this

Sit down with the owner (call or in person) and walk through the report together. Confirm, in their own words, what "more calls" needs to mean for month two — write that down, it's the north star for everything in the next 30 days.
