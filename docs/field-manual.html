01

Start here

The five-minute version of the whole business, before anything else.

What First Call is: a local SEO / AI-visibility agency based in Calgary, Alberta, serving local and regional Canadian home-service businesses — HVAC, roofing, mold & remediation, cleaning, auto & wraps, trucking, and similar owner-led trades. The pitch: most of these businesses lose jobs not because they do worse work, but because a competitor is easier to find on Google Maps, in AI answers, and by voice. First Call fixes the findability.

What you actually sell right now: one product, called Foundation — CAD $2,799 one-time setup, then CAD $139.99/month, month-to-month. That's it. Growth and Domination tiers exist on paper (see the process) but aren't for sale yet — don't offer them.

The domain is changing — read this before you send anyone a link. firstcallmarketing.ai is not recoverable — it's registered and DNS-hosted on accounts nobody here has access to (confirmed 2026-08-25), and it's frozen on an old, unrelated snapshot of the site. The plan is to buy firstcallconsulting.ai (confirmed available, $160/2yr) and use that instead — not purchased yet as of this writing. Everywhere this manual says firstcallmarketing.ai below, mentally substitute whatever the live domain actually is by the time you're reading this. Full story in the Readiness report linked in the sidebar.

Read this before you talk to a prospect: some of what's "built" runs on placeholder data until real API keys are configured — the live audit score is always real, but keyword volumes and competitor numbers are illustrative until a paid data source is connected, and research briefs are rule-based (not written by Claude) until an Anthropic key is set. Never present a placeholder number as verified. The site itself already enforces this honesty in its own copy — match that tone with prospects.

02

The software stack

Five systems. What each one actually does, and — just as important — what it doesn't.

1. The website firstcallmarketing.ai ↗

The public marketing site: homepage, six industry pages, five service pages, four resource guides, pricing, and legal pages. Its job is to get a trades business owner to run the free audit or talk to a strategist. It is also the checkout — /start is where a visitor becomes a paying client.

2. The audit scanner /audit ↗

This is real. It fetches the prospect's actual homepage plus up to three interior pages and their robots.txt, then runs about 25 checks across five surfaces — Google & Maps, AI Overviews, AI Assistants, Voice, and Website & Reviews — and returns a score out of 100. No AI, no cost, no placeholder data. This is your diagnostic tool and your lead magnet at the same time.

For an in-person or live-call pitch, use /admin/generate instead of the public form — it runs the same real scan plus the full research + proposal pipeline against a named prospect, on the spot, and gives you something printable to hand them.

3. SEO delivery software — the two agents

Once someone pays, two AI agents run automatically: a Research agent that uses the real audit plus keyword/competitor tools to write a structured brief, and an Implementation agent that turns that brief into a 90-day plan (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 days) with quick wins and KPIs. The output becomes the client's report and proposal.

Two modes: with a real ANTHROPIC_API_KEY configured, both agents are genuinely written by Claude. Without one — the default — a free rule-based engine produces the same shape of output instantly, at zero cost, clearly marked as such internally. The system will never auto-send a mock report to a paying client as if it were the real thing; it flags it for you to regenerate. Whoever handles the "deal closed" notification needs to know to check for that flag.

4. "Website creation" — read this one carefully

There is no software that builds or generates a client's website. This does not exist yet, and it isn't part of Foundation.

Foundation optimizes a client's existing web presence — Business Profile, on-page content, schema, reviews. It explicitly does not include building a new site from scratch. If a prospect's site is so broken it needs to be rebuilt, or they don't have one at all, that is not something to promise as part of the standard offer.

What to do instead: treat it as custom, separately-scoped work. Pass it to Josh directly — don't quote a price or timeline for it yourself until there's a real process for it. It's fine to tell a prospect "we can scope that separately" and move the conversation back to Foundation.

5. The CRM /crm ↗

Where every lead lives once it exists — see Leads for the full walkthrough. Login-gated: you (the manager) see every lead and every employee's numbers; each employee sees only their own assigned leads. Not the public site — nothing here is indexed or linked from the marketing pages.

03

Leads

Live in the CRM now. Empty today only because no real leads have loaded in yet.

Every lead — however it arrives — lives in one place: firstcallmarketing.ai/crm/leads, behind a login. There's no separate spreadsheet or file to check anymore; if it's not in the CRM, it isn't tracked.

Where a lead comes from decides its starting status

SourceHow it gets inStarting status

Status after that is automatic, not something you set: the moment anyone logs a contact against a lead — a call, email, text, meeting, or even just a note — it flips to hot, regardless of where it came from. It stays hot until someone marks it won or lost from the lead's own page. There's no way to manually mark a lead cold or warm — that would defeat the point of the system telling you, at a glance, who's actually been touched.

What you can do from a lead's page (its "customer profile")

  • See contact info, trade, source, and full history at a glance.
  • Log a contact — pick the type (call/email/text/meeting/note) and write what happened. This is the single action that turns a name on a list into an actual worked lead.
  • Read every past contact, newest first — so anyone picking up a lead cold can see exactly what's already been said.
  • Keep freeform notes separate from the contact log, for anything that doesn't belong in a timeline.
  • (Manager only) Assign or reassign the lead to any employee.
  • Mark it won or lost once the outcome is known.

Logging in for the first time

You (the manager) create your own account once, at /crm/setup — it only works while the CRM has zero accounts, so nobody else can do this after you. From then on, create each employee's account yourself from /crm/employees, including their first password — hand it to them and have them change it after they sign in.

04

The full process, start to finish

Every step from "never heard of us" to "active, reported-on client." This is the sequence — follow it in order.

  1. Find or receive the prospect
    Check /crm/leads first — website submissions and closed deals already land there automatically. If you found someone yourself (referral, outreach, in person), add them from /crm/leads/new the moment you know they exist, even before you've spoken to them — that's what keeps them warm instead of untracked.
  2. Run the real audit
    Either they've already run /audit themselves, or you run it for them live via /admin/generate during a call or in person. Either way, you now have a real, defensible score out of 100 to talk from.
  3. Walk them through the score
    Use it as a diagnostic, not a scare tactic. Show what's failing across Maps, AI Overviews, AI Assistants, Voice, and Website/Reviews, and connect each failure to a real consequence: a missed emergency call, a competitor named instead of them in an AI answer. Never claim a specific ranking or traffic outcome — the audit measures signals, not guarantees.
  4. Present Foundation
    CAD $2,799 one-time setup + CAD $139.99/month. What's included: Business Profile + local optimization, a review acquisition system, core on-page + LocalBusiness schema, foundational content, plain-English monthly reporting, and a senior strategist owning the account. What's not included: paid ad spend, full multi-location AEO/GEO, digital PR, or custom website builds.
  5. Handle objections honestly
    See How to close for specific language. The short version: don't oversell. The MSA itself says no guaranteed rank, call volume, or revenue — sell the system and the ownership, not a promise.
  6. Send them to checkout
    firstcallmarketing.ai/start — they enter business name, website, trade, and email, then continue to Stripe. Paying is how they agree to the MSA and Foundation SOW (linked right on that page). If Stripe isn't configured yet, the page instead shows an "email us for an invoice" fallback — see Collecting payment.
  7. Payment clears
    Stripe confirms the checkout, First Call's system responds instantly, then automatically — in the background — runs both delivery agents and saves a report tied to that client. This step needs zero manual action from you unless the mock-report flag comes up (see below).
  8. First deliverable is generated
    The research brief and 90-day plan are built and saved automatically. If it was built by the real Claude agents, the client gets emailed their report link directly. If it ran on the free mock engine (no API key configured), the client is not emailed — you get an internal note flagged "MOCK DATA," meaning someone needs to regenerate it for real before the client ever sees it.
  9. Onboard the client
    Request access: Google Business Profile, analytics, and CMS/website login, plus one point of contact. This is asked for directly in the "deal closed" email template already built into the system — you're just following up on it.
  10. Assign a senior strategist and start month one
    Foundation is explicitly senior-strategist-owned, not junior/outsourced. Use the client-facing Month-1 playbook (/playbook) as the execution checklist — it's already written and public.
  11. Report monthly, in plain English
    Calls, jobs, and visibility — not vanity metrics. This is a stated commitment in Foundation's included scope; build the habit of a monthly touchpoint from client zero.
  12. Renewal / cancellation
    Month-to-month. Either side can cancel with 30 days' written notice to First Call's contact email. No annual lock-in, no early-termination fee. Already-paid fees for the current month aren't refunded except where required by law.

How to close

The tone that's already built into the brand — use it, don't invent a different one.

First Call's own voice, straight from the site: "Be the first call." Confident, plain-spoken, allergic to hype. Sell the system and the ownership — not a guaranteed outcome. That restraint is itself part of the pitch: most agencies overpromise, and a trades business owner has heard every one of those pitches before.

A working script

Opening, after showing the audit score
"Here's exactly where you're losing calls right now — not opinions, this is what's actually on your site and profile today. Most of what's broken here is fixable. Foundation is $2,799 to set up, then $139.99 a month, month-to-month — no contract lock-in. You get a senior strategist, not a junior account manager, and a plain-English report every month on calls, jobs, and visibility, not vanity metrics."
If they ask "will I rank #1?"
"I won't promise a specific rank or call volume — nobody honest can, and our contract says so in writing. What I can tell you is exactly what we're fixing and why it moves the signals that decide who gets the call."
If they ask for a new website
"Foundation optimizes what you've already got — it doesn't include building a new site from scratch. If yours genuinely needs a rebuild, that's a separate, custom conversation — let's start with Foundation and scope that piece separately."

Objection: "I want to think about it"

Don't discount. Offer the standalone Technical SEO Audit (CAD $497–$997) as a lower-commitment entry point if Foundation genuinely feels too big a first step — it exists precisely for this, though it isn't wired up for self-serve checkout yet, so it's a manual invoice today.

Collecting payment

Two paths, depending on whether Stripe is configured. Know both.

Path A — Stripe Checkout (normal path)

  1. Client fills the form at /start: business name, website, trade, email.
  2. The system creates one Stripe Checkout session containing both prices — the one-time $2,799 setup fee and the $139.99/month retainer — billed together as a single subscription. The setup fee charges once, on the first invoice; the retainer continues monthly.
  3. Client pays on Stripe's hosted page (First Call never touches card details directly).
  4. Stripe redirects them to a "payment received" confirmation page.
  5. Stripe notifies First Call's system in the background, which kicks off report generation and the onboarding email automatically — see step 7 of the process.

Requires the two Stripe prices to already exist in the Stripe dashboard, plus the secret key and webhook secret configured — see the Readiness report's punch list.

Path B — manual invoice (fallback)

If Stripe isn't configured, /start and /pricing both show the same message instead of a checkout button: email the contact address with business, website, and trade, and First Call sends a Stripe invoice or payment link directly. Use this path knowingly — don't let a prospect hit a dead end wondering if the form is broken.

Compliance & legal

This is a Canadian business — Canadian rules apply, not US ones.

CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation)

  • Every commercial email needs consent — express, or clearly implied through an existing business relationship. Never a purchased or scraped list.
  • Every commercial email must identify the sender and include a real mailing address.
  • Every commercial email needs a working, immediate unsubscribe. This is already built — one-click, signed so nobody can unsubscribe someone else's address by guessing a link.
  • Penalties run up to CAD $10M per violation for a business. This is not a formality.

PIPEDA

Collect only the personal data you need, say what it's for, protect it, and let people access, correct, or delete their own data on request.

The contracts

The MSA and Foundation SOW cover term (month-to-month, 30 days' notice to cancel), fees, ownership of deliverables, client responsibilities (timely access, honest information, no fake or gated reviews), confidentiality, and a liability cap set to fees paid in the prior three months. Neither has been reviewed by a lawyer yet — the documents say so themselves. Don't treat them as final until that review happens.

Glossary

For a new hire with no SEO background. Plain English, no jargon left unexplained.

Map Pack
The top three local business results Google shows above regular search results, with a map. Most local clicks go here.
GBP
Google Business Profile — the free listing that controls what shows in the Map Pack (hours, reviews, category, photos).
NAP
Name, Address, Phone — must match exactly everywhere it appears online, or search engines trust the listing less.
Local SEO
The work of ranking well for "near me" and city-specific searches — mostly GBP optimization, reviews, and citations.
AEO
Answer Engine Optimization — structuring content so Google's AI Overviews can quote it directly instead of just linking to a competitor.
GEO
Generative Engine Optimization — the same idea, but for being cited inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini answers.
Schema
Hidden code on a page that tells search engines and AI exactly what a business is, where it is, and what it offers — machine-readable, not something a visitor sees.
E-E-A-T
Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness — Google's quality framework. Real photos, named credentialed people, and accurate facts score higher than generic template copy.
Core Web Vitals
Google's page-speed/stability metrics. A slow or janky site ranks worse, independent of content quality.
Retainer
The recurring monthly fee for ongoing work, as opposed to the one-time setup fee.
Churn
A client canceling. Foundation has no lock-in, so retention depends entirely on visible results and the monthly report.
